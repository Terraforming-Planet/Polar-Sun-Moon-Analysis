L4 Training #2 — Site Corpus Findings and Evidence

The second training run was a deliberate expansion from the narrow 66-image baseline into the research material already published by Terraforming Planet. It combined local project assets with project-gallery satellite imagery, audited every readable unique image on the GPU, and then ran a full 60-minute self-supervised denoising pretrain. This page records the hardware, corpus composition, measured optimization behavior, what was and was not compared, and the lessons that directly motivated the much larger streaming Training #3.

What the run actually taught us

FINDING-01DERIVED_VALUE

The full site corpus passed the GPU audit without unreadable inputs

All 290 of 290 unique images were processed by the CUDA audit, with 0 unreadable files. That establishes that the collected research corpus was technically usable by the training pipeline at 512 px.

Supports: corpus readability and end-to-end GPU ingestion. Does not support: scientific correctness of labels or environmental causation.

FINDING-02DERIVED_VALUE

The denoising objective improved strongly over a full hour

Loss fell from 0.337373 to 0.072435, a 78.53% reduction. The best observed loss was 0.037302, which is 88.94% below the initial value.

Supports: stable optimization on the broader site corpus. Does not support: accuracy of water, glacier, river or drought detection.

FINDING-03DERIVED_VALUE

Training #2 materially broadened source diversity relative to Training #1

The source corpus grew from 66 images in Training #1 to 290 unique images in Training #2 — a 4.39× larger corpus, or roughly +339%. It also introduced explicit project-region coverage including Grays Harbor, the Vistula and Himalaya–Tibet.

Operational lesson: the project moved from a narrow baseline toward geographically identifiable research material.

FINDING-04MODEL_ESTIMATE

Repeated sampling was still heavy enough that generalization remained a concern

The run produced 696,312 optimization samples from 290 unique source images, averaging about 2,401 sampled training instances per source image. This is much healthier than Run #1's narrower corpus, but it still does not replace held-out scene validation.

Interpretation: this is a methodological risk assessment, not a measured overfitting rate.

FINDING-05UNKNOWN

No site-level change magnitude can be claimed from Training #2 alone

The training objective reconstructed/denoised images. It did not persist deterministic water masks, shoreline vectors, glacier outlines, snow fractions, vegetation indices or matched before/after change metrics for each scene pair.

Therefore: this run prepared the model and audited imagery, but it cannot honestly be cited as proof that a specific lake lost X hectares or a river narrowed by Y metres.

FINDING-06DERIVED_VALUE

The result directly justified the streaming design of Training #3

Training #2 proved that the project could train stably on a broader site corpus, but 290 unique images was still far below the intended global scale. That limitation led to Training #3, where new NASA GIBS geospatial/time windows were streamed continuously instead of replaying only a static local corpus.

Design progression: 66-image baseline → 290-image site corpus → 200k-window streaming run.

Scientific interpretation

Training #2 was successful as a training and data-engineering experiment. It demonstrated one-hour CUDA stability on Windows/NVIDIA L4, complete GPU readability of a 290-image research corpus, and strong improvement of the self-supervised denoising objective. It also exposed the next scientific requirement: training success is not the same as Earth-change measurement. Reliable environmental conclusions require a separate deterministic analysis pass with matched dates/seasons, scene provenance, cloud/quality control and explicit pixel-derived measurements.

TP-26 evidence policy: OBSERVATION / DERIVED_VALUE / MODEL_ESTIMATE / HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN are kept distinct. Training counters are not silently promoted into environmental findings.
